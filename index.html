Craftsmanship

Every trunk, tusk and thorn tree is cut by hand — no moulds, no repeats.

Our carvers start with a rough sketch chalked directly onto the slab, then work in from the outer rope border, hollowing the background before raising the animals in relief. A single elephant-family chair back can take a carver the better part of a week.

Wood comes from managed jacaranda, mahogany and mvule off-cuts sourced around Nairobi and the central highlands — nothing is felled to order.

Signature detail

The trunk always finds the calf.

It's the detail our regulars ask for by name — a mother elephant's trunk curled down toward her calf, carved so the two pieces of wood seem to touch without ever being joined.

How a piece is made

From offcut to finished chair.

Four stages, roughly a fortnight, entirely inside the yard .

Stage 1

Slab & sketch

A hardwood off-cut is squared off and the scene is chalked freehand onto the face.

Stage 2

Hollow & rough-out

Background wood is cut away first, leaving the animals and trees standing proud.

Stage 3

Relief & texture

Hide, bark and thatch textures are chiselled in by hand, panel by panel.

Stage 4

Sand & oil

A final sanding and three coats of oil bring out the grain before it leaves the yard.
